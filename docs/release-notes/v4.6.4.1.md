## What's changed

`Net.Agora.Video.Android`, `Net.Agora.Voice.Android` and the twelve `Net.Agora.Extensions.*.Android`
packages advance to **4.6.4.1**, on `io.agora.rtc:*` **4.6.4** from Maven Central. The binding revision
resets to 1 with the new native line. 4.6.4 is the newest Android release on Maven; 4.7.0 has not
reached it.

`io.agora.infra:aosl` stays at 1.3.5, which is what the 4.6.4 `.pom` files ask for, and it exports every
aosl symbol the 4.6.4 engines import. `Net.Agora.Signaling.Android` therefore doesn't need a new
release: 2.2.6.3 keeps sharing one aosl with these packages.

### What Agora changed

Agora publishes no Android changelog in the artifact, so this comes from comparing 4.6.3 with 4.6.4.

- `AgoraVideoFrame` gains a `MetadataBuffer` field for pushed external video.
- Two local video events report the camera focal length:
  `LocalVideoEventTypeCameraFocalLengthApplied` and `LocalVideoEventTypeCameraFocalLengthFallbackToDefault`.
- The software video encoder is packaged differently. `full-video-codec-enc` folds `libvideo_enc.so`
  into `libagora_video_encoder_extension.so`, so the `.aar` halves from 3.8 MB to 1.9 MB.
  `Net.Agora.Extensions.VideoEncoder.Android` carries what the native artifact carries, and needs no
  change in an app.

### .NET API changes

Additive, apart from two constructors in the SDK's own internals:

- `Agora.Rtc.Internal.AudioDeviceInventoryLowerThanM` and `AudioDeviceInventoryMorHigher` now take a
  `Handler` as well as a `Context`. They are the SDK's internal audio-routing classes, not something
  an app constructs, so there is no compatibility shim.

### Samples

`samples/Net.Agora.Sample.Voice.Android` now takes an optional channel and token and joins that
channel, so it can meet its iOS sibling. Every sample can also start with its fields filled in from
build-time values. See `samples/Directory.Build.targets`. Nothing is filled in by default.

## Tested

Before release, on Android emulators: device suites on net8 and net10, with R8 shrinking, and with
Signaling in the same app. The Video and Voice samples ran live in a real channel with an iOS client,
exchanging video and audio, with garbage collections forced throughout the session.
